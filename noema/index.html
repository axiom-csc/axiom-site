Could we see the answer change coming?

Investigating why an answer changed—and whether the preceding conversation gave us reason to expect it.

The question before the next reply

Detecting that an assistant changed its answer is different from anticipating the change. Noema studies the evidence available in a conversation before a future reply is observed.

The research question is whether features of the preceding exchange can support a useful forecast of concession risk. A useful forecast must be evaluated against actual future behavior, rather than an explanation written after the outcome is known.

Separate pressure from information

Repeated requests, claimed authority, and emotional appeals can alter the conversation without altering the underlying policy. A valid correction or an explicit policy change can alter what the assistant should say. Forecasting has to preserve that distinction.

The benchmark’s separate run types provide a way to study it. The eventual analysis needs to examine both missed concessions and false alarms on legitimate updates.

Behavior is not an activation measurement

A forecast from conversation text is a behavioral prediction. It does not demonstrate that an internal activation direction has been found, nor that intervening on a model will change its behavior.

Spine studies that separate internal-mechanism question. Lemma evaluates departures after the conversation.

Early research

Forecasting reliability remains an open question. Noema is early research; this page does not claim a validated predictive performance level or a public installation package.

The conversation forecaster is separate from the world-model component also named Noema in Manam’s experimental implementation.
