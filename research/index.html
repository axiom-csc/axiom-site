Results so far

All numbers are from committed results files in the axiom-lab repository. Each experiment had its pass/fail rules written before it ran.

Null resultCross-world transfer

Solving chemistry tasks first did not make social tasks easier.

We tested whether program-search tools learned in a simulated biochemistry world would cut the search needed on held-out tasks in a simulated social world. Without biochemistry, held-out search took 40,198 evaluations; with it, 40,206 (5 seeds). No reusable abstractions were learned. The pre-registered decision: no demonstrated transfer benefit at this task scale. We have not shown that the system learns to reason across worlds.

Missed criteriaPhysics rule learning

It learned most physics rules on held-out runs, but missed the bar.

On sealed holdout runs the engine got at least 7 of 8 ball-physics rules right in 8 of 10 runs, with 39 parameters on average. Its mean rollout error was 0.107 m against a pre-set limit of 0.05 m, so the learning criterion failed. The tests for recovering after the world’s rules change, and for beating small transformer baselines on that, also failed.

NegativeSearch baseline

Neither our solver nor a genetic-programming baseline solved the hardest task.

On a pre-registered held-out task, our hybrid search solver was correct in 0 of 5 seeds and the genetic-programming baseline in 0 of 5. We report this as it is: no winner.

MeasurementCode graph

Parsing a large real codebase into a call graph.

The code parser behind CallGuard read the full Django repository in about 26 seconds, producing 46,520 entities and 65,486 relations. Blast-radius queries took a median of about 84 ms. This is one machine and one run, not a general speed claim.
