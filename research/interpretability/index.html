Spine

Can an internal signal help distinguish giving in to pressure from accepting a valid correction?

Research by Naren SaravananResearch overview · November 2026

On this page

A hypothesis inside the modelPreserve the ability to updateAn experiment, not an established result

A hypothesis inside the model

Spine investigates whether an activation direction is associated with concessions under conversational pressure. The hypothesis is about a measurable internal representation, not just a change in the final text.

There are two separate questions: whether a direction predicts concessions, and whether changing that direction changes behavior in a useful way. A correlation alone does not answer the intervention question.

Preserve the ability to update

An intervention that prevents every answer change would not solve the research problem. It must reduce concessions under illegitimate pressure while preserving responses to valid corrections and explicit policy changes.

Our evaluation design therefore separates those conditions. The tradeoff between holding a rule and accepting evidence is the central test.

An experiment, not an established result

Spine is early research. We are not claiming that a robust “caving direction” has been established, that an intervention generalizes across models, or that the proposed tradeoff has been solved.
