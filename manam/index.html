Architecture / Early research

Manam

An experimental non-LLM architecture for learning rules, making predictions, and revising a model when evidence changes.

Research by Naren SaravananResearch overview · November 2026

On this page

One representation, connected worlds

Manam investigates whether learning across different worlds can be supported by a common representation and one shared library of rules. The implementation represents entities, typed attributes, and relations as a graph. Observations from a world enter that representation; candidate rules are evaluated against those observations.

The research question is whether a useful abstraction learned in one setting can help in another while preserving the conditions under which it is valid. That transfer is an objective to test, not an established capability.

How the architecture fits together

The rule-learning implementation uses typed programs and description-length scoring. Search combines forward enumeration with backward search. Candidate abstractions are checked against task examples before they can enter the shared library.

Revision begins with a failed prediction

A mismatch between a prediction and an observation can mean several things: a parameter changed, a rule no longer applies, or the model was wrong. The implementation first attempts to refit a rule’s constants. It searches for a new structure if refitting cannot explain the evidence.

Revision is gated by accumulated surprising observations, with a record of the old rule, the replacement, and the reason for the change. This makes it possible to inspect what was revised instead of treating every update as an undifferentiated improvement.

What we are testing

The research implementation includes toy list tasks, physical worlds, experiment selection, and inverse-planning components. These provide controlled settings for studying rule search, abstraction, prediction, and revision.

Controlled experiments make failure visible: a short program can fit every training example and still express the wrong rule. Abstraction can also appear to help when a simpler change in search priorities explains most of the gain. These are reasons to compare against baselines and inspect held-out behavior.

Current scope

Manam is early-stage research. Results from controlled tasks do not establish general intelligence, broad cross-domain transfer, or reliable reasoning about people. Computational efficiency is a design constraint; measured deployment requirements and an installation package are not yet published.

The conversation tools Lemma and Noema are separate projects from the similarly named internal rule-learning and world-model components in this architecture.
