Lemma

A conversation evaluator for finding where an assistant’s answer departed from a stated policy.

Research by Naren SaravananResearch overview · November 2026

From a conversation to a specific failure

A final answer can conceal the path that produced it. An assistant may initially follow a rule, then reverse course after a false claim, a repeated demand, or an appeal to authority. Lemma is being developed to locate that change in the conversation.

The evaluation starts with a stated policy. It then examines the assistant’s replies in context, distinguishing a concession under pressure from a response to a legitimate correction.

A policy is part of the test

The same reply can be appropriate under one policy and wrong under another. Lemma’s task is therefore tied to the explicit rule supplied with a scenario. The policy, turn sequence, and reason for changing an answer belong together in the evaluation.

Our pressure-ladder benchmark includes escalating pressure, valid-correction runs, and policy-change runs. These controls matter: a system that refuses every update would hold a policy under pressure while failing to respond to evidence.

What a judgment can tell us

A conversation-level judgment can identify a behavioral departure and the turn where it occurred. It cannot, on its own, establish which internal mechanism caused the departure. That question belongs to separate interpretability experiments.

Noema investigates a related forecasting question: whether the preceding conversation contains evidence that an answer is about to change.

Development status

Lemma is in development. The benchmark methodology page records the planned dataset structure and release status. Detailed judge validation and error analysis belong in the forthcoming technical report.

This conversation evaluator is separate from the internal rule-learning component also named Lemma in the experimental Manam architecture.
